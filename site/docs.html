After you buy — where your product arrives#

Nothing downloads in the browser when a payment goes through. There is no account to log into and no download button that appears on this site. Everything you paid for arrives by email, at the address you typed into checkout, so the first thing to check after a purchase is that inbox.

Two messages arrive, and they are not the same thing:

  • The receipt, from the payment provider. It confirms the money was taken and carries the payment reference. This is the record of the purchase — keep it, because every later question about the order is answered fastest by replying to it.
  • The delivery email from us, to the same address: the installer download links for macOS, Windows and Linux, and the licence file licence.sslic attached.

The licence file is the product. There is no iLok, no dongle and no activation code to type in — the plug-ins run unauthorised until that one small file is in place, then they run fully. Drop it onto any open SOUNDSHOP plug-in window, or put it in the per-platform folder listed under Authorisation. If you bought the Full Shop bundle, the same file authorises all four instruments; you do not get one file per plug-in.

If the plugins page told you the payment was still being confirmed rather than received, nothing has been sent yet and nothing more is needed from you. Some methods, bank transfer among them, settle after you leave the checkout. The receipt still arrives; the installers and licence file follow once the payment clears. Do not pay a second time — a duplicate charge is refunded, but that takes longer than waiting.

Nothing has arrived

Work through this before assuming the purchase failed:

  • Check spam, promotions and any tab your mail client sorts into. An email carrying an attachment and download links is exactly what aggressive filters hold back.
  • Check the address you actually used at checkout — it is printed on the receipt, and a typo there is the single most common reason a delivery never lands.
  • Look at the banner on the plugins page you were returned to after paying. It states whether the payment was received, cancelled or still being confirmed, and it shows the payment reference.
  • Still nothing: reply to the receipt email, quoting that reference. It reaches the same people who answer everything else — see Support. A cancelled checkout means no money was taken and no product is owed; nothing was charged.

Installation and authorisation#

Every SOUNDSHOP installer contains all four formats for your platform and lets you deselect the ones you do not use. Nothing is installed outside the standard plug-in directories and one small support folder. There is no background service, no helper daemon, and nothing that phones home at load time.

macOS 11 or newer (Universal)

Open the disk image and run SOUNDSHOP Installer.pkg. The installer is notarised, so Gatekeeper will not prompt. Formats land in the system-wide locations, which means every user account on the machine sees them:

Getting started with VANTA#

VANTA is an eight-voice virtual-analog polysynth. Two oscillators and a sub feed one filter block per voice, then an amplifier, then a shared effects section. There is no hidden routing anywhere in the instrument: what the panel shows is what the signal does, in that order.

The fastest way to understand it is to open the playable demo, which runs the same parameter set in your browser, and follow along:

  1. Load the plug-in on an instrument track and play a held chord. The factory init patch is a two-saw sound with a moderate filter envelope — deliberately plain, so you can hear what you change.
  2. Pull Cutoff down until the chord goes dull, then raise Env Amount. You are now hearing the filter envelope open each voice independently, not one shared filter.
  3. Raise Detune from 12 to about 30 cents. The spread is split symmetrically between oscillator 1 and oscillator 2, so the centre pitch does not move as you widen it.
  4. Set LFO Target to Filter and give it a little depth. Rate is logarithmic, so the bottom half of the control is where the slow movement lives.
  5. Turn the Arpeggiator on and hold the same chord. Notes are scheduled ahead of time against the audio clock rather than a timer, so the pattern does not drift under CPU load.
  6. Save what you have. Presets are plain text — see Preset management.

The one thing worth knowing early: voices are stolen in a defined order when a ninth note arrives. A free slot first, then the oldest voice that is already in its release tail, and only then the oldest held voice. That is why a sustained chord keeps its tails while you play over the top of it.

VANTA parameter reference#

Forty parameters, listed in signal order, with the exact range, step and default the engine uses. The name in the left column is also the automation ID your host sees (see MIDI learn & automation) and the key you use in a preset file.

Three parameters are mapped logarithmically — cutoff, lfoRate and delayDamp. A linear control over those ranges would put nine tenths of the useful travel in the first tenth of the sweep, so they are exponential end to end.

Filter#

Filter parameters
ParameterRangeDefaultWhat it does
Filter Type filtType Low Pass · High Pass · Band Pass Low Pass Response of the per-voice filter. Every voice has its own; nothing is shared across the polyphony.
Cutoff cutoff 30 – 18 000 Hz, step 1 (log) 1 200 Hz Corner frequency. Mapped exponentially, so the bottom of the sweep has as much resolution as the top. Modulation from the envelope, key tracking and the LFO is summed on top of this value.
Resonance reso 0.10 – 24.0 Q, step 0.1 3.5 Q Feedback around the filter. Above roughly 18 the peak is loud enough to dominate the voice; pull oscillator levels back rather than reaching for the master.
Env Amount fegAmt −4800 – +4800 cents, step 10 +2200 cents Bipolar depth of the filter envelope, applied through the filter's detune input so the sweep is exponential and musical rather than linear in Hz. Negative values invert the envelope, which is how you get a filter that closes on the attack.

Filter envelope#

A four-stage envelope per voice, scaled by fegAmt and added to the cutoff. Attack is a linear ramp; decay and release are exponential with a guaranteed hard landing, so nothing is left ringing below the noise floor.

Filter envelope parameters
ParameterRangeDefaultWhat it does
Filter Attack fegA0 – 4.000 s, step 0.0010.020 sTime from note-on to full envelope. At 0 the sweep is instantaneous and you hear the click of the filter opening, which is often what you want on a bass.
Filter Decay fegD0.001 – 6.000 s, step 0.0010.550 sFall time from the peak down to the sustain level. This is the control that makes a pluck a pluck.
Filter Sustain fegS0 – 1.00, step 0.010.25Held level as a fraction of Env Amount. 0 means the filter returns fully closed while the key is still down.
Filter Release fegR0.005 – 8.000 s, step 0.0050.600 sFall time after note-off, starting from wherever the envelope actually was — releasing during the attack does not jump.

Amp envelope#

The same shape, controlling the voice amplifier. The amp envelope also decides when a voice is returned to the pool: the slot is freed after the release tail has actually finished, not when the key comes up.

Amp envelope parameters
ParameterRangeDefaultWhat it does
Amp Attack ampA0 – 4.000 s, step 0.0010.020 sFade-in time. Values under about 5 ms read as a transient rather than a fade.
Amp Decay ampD0.001 – 6.000 s, step 0.0010.600 sFall from full level to the sustain level.
Amp Sustain ampS0 – 1.00, step 0.010.70Held level while the key is down. At 0 the sound is percussive and the release control does nothing audible.
Amp Release ampR0.005 – 8.000 s, step 0.0050.700 sTail after note-off. Long releases plus fast playing is the usual cause of voice stealing — see CPU & latency.

LFO#

One LFO, free-running, routed to a single destination. Depth is normalised, and the amount it represents depends on where it is going: at full depth, pitch moves ±120 cents, the filter moves ±3600 cents, and amplitude moves ±0.6. Amp modulation runs on a dedicated per-voice gain, so a silent voice stays silent.

LFO parameters
ParameterRangeDefaultWhat it does
LFO Wave lfoWaveSine · Triangle · Square · Sample and HoldTriangleModulation shape. Sample and hold re-randomises at the rate below, which on the filter is the fastest route to a broken-machine texture.
LFO Rate lfoRate0.02 – 20.00 Hz, step 0.01 (log)4.20 HzSpeed. Exponential across the range: the lower half is 0.02–1 Hz for slow drift, the upper half runs into audible-rate territory.
LFO Depth lfoDepth0 – 1.00, step 0.010.08Modulation amount, scaled per destination as described above.
LFO Target lfoTargetOff · Pitch · Filter · AmpFilterDestination. Changing target releases the old destination cleanly rather than leaving it parked at its last modulated value.

Effects#

The effects run once on the summed voices, not per voice. Delay and reverb are parallel sends — the dry path stays at unity, so raising a mix control adds signal instead of trading it away.

Arpeggiator#

With the arpeggiator on, held notes go into a pool instead of sounding directly. Steps are scheduled ahead against the audio clock with a lookahead window, not fired from a timer, so the pattern stays rigid under load. Turning it off re-sounds anything still held.

Arp Tempo arpBpm40 – 240 BPM, step 1112 BPMInternal tempo, used when there is no host transport to sync to.
Arp Octaves arpOct1 – 3 oct, step 11How many octaves the pattern climbs before repeating.

Computer keyboard (browser demo)#

The in-browser demo maps your typing keyboard to two octaves, laid out like a piano: the home row is the white keys, the row above it is the black keys. The mapping is a QWERTY layout by position, so it is the same shape on any keyboard where those keys sit where they normally sit.

Octave range is 0 to 8 and the default is 4, so A is middle C (MIDI note 60). Key repeat is ignored, so holding a key sustains one note rather than retriggering it. Browsers will only start audio inside a real click or keypress — the first thing you do in the demo has to be a deliberate gesture, which is why there is a power button.

Browser demo

Audio quality and voice count are limited by the browser; the plug-in is not. The demo is the same parameter set and the same architecture, running on Web Audio with an eight-voice ceiling.

Preset management#

Presets are plain UTF-8 JSON, one file per patch, in a folder you choose. They are diffable, they survive version control, and you can paste one into a chat window and have it load at the other end. Nothing is stored in a database and nothing is indexed behind your back.

Default user preset folder per platform
PlatformDefault user preset folder
macOS~/Music/Soundshop/VANTA/Presets
Windows%USERPROFILE%\Documents\Soundshop\VANTA\Presets
Linux~/.config/soundshop/VANTA/presets

Factory presets sit alongside the plug-in bundle and are never written to. Saving over a factory name creates a user preset that shadows it; deleting yours brings the factory patch back.

The file shape

A preset is an object with an identifier, a name, an author, an optional category and tag list, and a params map. Every key in params is a parameter name from the reference table above, and the value is that parameter's value — a number for a range, the option string for an enum.

{
  "id": "falling-apart-slowly",
  "name": "Falling Apart Slowly",
  "author": "Soundshop",
  "category": "Pad",
  "tags": ["pad", "wide", "drift"],
  "description": "Wide two-saw pad with the drift turned up.",
  "params": {
    "osc1wave": "sawtooth",
    "osc2wave": "sawtooth",
    "detune": 34,
    "cutoff": 900,
    "reso": 6.5,
    "fegAmt": 1800,
    "ampA": 0.42,
    "ampR": 2.4,
    "lfoTarget": "cutoff",
    "lfoRate": 0.18,
    "lfoDepth": 0.22,
    "revMix": 0.44
  }
}

Partial presets are legal and useful. Any key you leave out keeps its current value, unknown keys are ignored, and out-of-range values are rejected rather than clamped into something surprising — so a "filter only" preset that touches four parameters is a perfectly valid file.

The same shape drives the preset gallery on this site and the browser demo, which reads a single JSON file holding an array of these objects. Copy any entry out of that array, save it as its own .json file in the folder above, and the plug-in will list it after a rescan.

Moving presets between machines

  • Export: the preset folder is the export. Copy it.
  • Import: drop .json files into the folder and hit rescan in the preset browser. No import dialog, no library rebuild.
  • Share one patch: the file is small enough to paste as text. It will load from a paste as long as the JSON is intact.
  • Version control: keys are written in a stable order, so a diff between two saves shows only what you actually changed.

MIDI learn and automation#

Right-click any control and choose MIDI Learn, then move a knob on your controller. The binding is stored with the session, and a second entry in the same menu stores it as the global default for every new instance. Forget removes it.

Automation IDs

Host-facing parameter IDs are the short names in the left column of the parameter reference — cutoff, fegAmt, ampR, arpGate and so on. They are stable: an ID has never been renamed or reused between versions, and it will not be. Automation written against 1.0 still lands on the same parameter in 1.4.0.

The display name your host shows is the human label ("Filter Attack"), and the value is formatted with its unit — seconds, cents, Hz, Q, BPM. Ranges in automation are normalised 0–1 by the host, and the two logarithmic parameters map exponentially across that range, so an automation line drawn straight across cutoff sweeps by ear rather than by arithmetic.

MPE and plain MIDI

  • MPE: per-note pitch bend, pressure and slide reach the voice they belong to. Enable it in the host; VANTA follows the zone configuration the host sends.
  • Legacy mode: the same destinations are driven by the mod wheel (CC 1) and channel aftertouch, so an ordinary keyboard reaches everything an MPE controller does, just not per note.
  • Sustain (CC 64): holds notes in the voice pool. With the arpeggiator on, held notes stay in the pattern until the pedal comes up.
  • All Notes Off (CC 123) and panic both stop every voice with a short ramp rather than a hard cut, so a stuck note never turns into a click.

CPU and latency#

Nothing we ship introduces latency you did not ask for. VANTA reports zero samples of latency in all four formats. ANVIL reports zero in its zero-latency mode and the oversampler's group delay in its oversampled mode, which the host compensates automatically. DRIFT and PRISM report zero.

Where the cycles go

  • Voice count is the main cost. Eight voices with long releases and fast playing means eight voices sounding almost all the time. Shortening Amp Release is the single biggest saving available in the instrument.
  • Drive oversamples ×4. If you are not using it, leave it low; if you are, it is worth the cycles and there is no cheaper way to get that curve without aliasing.
  • Resonance is not free at extreme settings, because the filter is solved with more headroom to stay stable near self-oscillation.
  • Reverb size changes rebuild the impulse response. The rebuild is debounced, so automating it slowly is fine and automating it at audio rate is not — use the mix control instead.
  • Buffer size beats everything else. Going from 64 to 256 samples typically halves the per-block overhead across a whole session. Track at 64–128, mix at 256–512.

When the meter is red

Freeze or bounce the track. Every parameter is automatable and every preset is a text file, so a frozen track is trivially un-frozen later with nothing lost. If a single instance is pinning a core on its own on modern hardware, that is a bug — tell us the patch and the host and we will look at it.

Troubleshooting#

The six things people actually write to us about, in order of how often.

My host does not see the plug-in after installing

Nine times out of ten the host is scanning a folder the installer did not write to. Check the paths under Installation against your host's plug-in scan list, add the correct folder, and force a rescan.

On macOS, Logic caches Audio Unit validation results — if a scan was interrupted, the plug-in is remembered as failed. Open Plug-in Manager, select it, and choose Reset & Rescan Selection. On Windows, check that you did not install to a VST2 folder: we do not ship VST2, so a VST2-only host will never find us.

A burst of noise every ninety seconds

That is demo mode. The licence file has not been found. Drag licence.sslic onto the plug-in window, or place it at the path listed under Authorisation and reopen the plug-in.

If the file is definitely there and demo mode persists, the licence was issued for a different machine fingerprint — that happens after a motherboard swap or a full OS reinstall. Send us the order reference and we will reissue.

Notes cut off when I play more than eight at once

Working as designed: VANTA has eight voices. The ninth note steals one, in this order — a free slot, then the oldest voice already in its release tail, then the oldest held voice.

If it happens sooner than you expect, look at Amp Release. A four-second release means a three-note phrase can be holding eight voices before you notice. Shorten the release, or use a second instance and split the part.

The arpeggiator drifts against the grid

In a host, the arpeggiator follows the host transport and cannot drift; arpBpm is only used when no transport is running. If your pattern sounds late, check that the track is not being delay-compensated against a plug-in further down the chain that reports latency.

In the browser demo there is no transport, so timing comes from arpBpm. Steps are scheduled ahead against the audio clock rather than fired from a timer, so a busy tab slows the animation but not the notes.

A preset loads but sounds wrong

Presets are partial by design: keys that are absent keep whatever value the instrument currently has. If you loaded a "filter only" patch on top of something else, that is exactly what you should hear.

Reset to the init patch first, then load. Values outside a parameter's range are rejected rather than clamped, so a hand-edited file with "reso": 400 silently keeps the previous resonance instead of exploding — check the file against the ranges table.

Crackles and dropouts under load

Raise the buffer size before anything else; 64 to 256 samples is usually the whole fix. Then read CPU and latency — long releases and heavy Drive are the two settings that cost the most.

On Windows, check that the audio interface is on its own USB controller and that power management is not parking cores. On macOS, an external display running at a high refresh rate shares bandwidth with some interfaces; unplugging it is a diagnostic, not a solution, but it tells you where to look.

Support#

Support is answered by the people who wrote the DSP. There is no tier-one script, no ticket deflection bot, and nobody will ask you to reinstall as a first move.

When something is wrong, the fastest possible message contains four things: your host and version, your OS and version, the plug-in and version (it is in the about panel), and the preset or a description of what you were doing. If it is a crash, the crash log; if it is a sound, a bounce. That is usually enough to reproduce it on our side the same day.

  • Check the changelog first — if you are one version behind, the fix may already be out, and every entry says what it changed and why.
  • Beta reports for PRISM 0.9.0 are welcome and wanted. The DSP is stable and the preset format is frozen; the interface is not, and that is the part we most want to hear about.
  • Licence problems — machine changes, transfers, reissues — are handled with an order reference and usually resolved in one reply.
