The rack

One instrument, three ways to wreck it

A polysynth to make the sound, a delay to move it in time, a filter to move it in frequency, and a dynamics stage to decide how hard it hits.

VANTA

8-voice virtual-analog polysynth

v1.4.0 Instrument·Polysynth

VANTA is our flagship: eight independent voices, each with two oscillators, a resonant ladder filter, and its own small amount of tuning and envelope error, because a perfectly matched voice card has never existed. Unison stacks all eight. The modulation matrix has twenty-four slots and no hidden routing. It is the instrument the playable demo on this site actually runs.

DRIFT

Tape-modelled delay and pitch-warping echo

v1.1.0 Effect·Delay

DRIFT models the parts of tape delay people actually miss: wow and flutter with correlated low-frequency drift, head-gap loss on repeats, and a record path that saturates before it distorts. Push the transport speed and the repeats bend with it, the way a machine does. Tempo-sync, free-run, and a freeze that holds the loop without re-recording into it.

PRISM

4-band spectral filter and formant morpher

Public beta 0.9.0 Effect·Spectral filter

PRISM splits the input into four spectral bands you can move, tilt and resonate independently, then lets you drag the formant structure of one band onto another. It is a public beta: the DSP is stable and the preset format is frozen, the interface is not. Buy it now at $69 and the 1.0 release is a free update. Report anything that misbehaves.

ANVIL

Transient shaper and saturating bus compressor

v2.0.1 Effect·Dynamics

ANVIL does two jobs in one signal path: a transient stage that separates attack from sustain without a detector to fight, and a bus compressor with program-dependent release and a saturation stage that runs after the gain element, not before it. Zero-latency mode for tracking, oversampled mode for mixdown. Version 2.0.1 is a free update for all 1.x owners.

Before you buy

The questions we actually get

Licensing, updates, and what a beta version number means when there is money involved.

Do I need an account to try anything?

No. The browser demo runs VANTA's DSP in this tab with nothing to sign up for and nothing to install. Downloadable demos of all four are unlimited in time and mute for two seconds every ninety; there is no account, no email gate and no telemetry in the demo builds.

How does the licence work?

You get a licence file. Drop it in the plugin's licence folder and it authorises three machines. There is no dongle, no iLok, and no online check when the plugin loads — a session opened on a plane behaves exactly like a session opened in the room. Licences are transferable: if you sell one, mail us and we move it.

What does “public beta” mean for PRISM?

It means the DSP and the preset format are done and the interface is not. PRISM 0.9.0 is safe to finish records with — sessions written today reload correctly at 1.0 — but control names, layout and a few parameter ranges will still move before the release. Buying at $69 gets you 1.0 free. In exchange we would like to hear about anything that misbehaves.

Do I pay again for updates?

Not inside a major version. ANVIL 2.0.1 was free for every 1.x owner, and every 1.x release of VANTA, DRIFT and PRISM is free for existing licences. When something is rebuilt from the ground up rather than improved, it gets a new major number and a new price, and we say so in the changelog rather than in a mail-out.

I already own one. Can I still get the bundle?

Yes — what you paid for the plugin you own comes off the bundle price, so nobody pays for the same DSP twice. Details, and the rest of the licensing terms, are on the documentation page.

Which plugin does the playable demo cover?

VANTA, and only VANTA. The in-browser demo runs a genuine eight-voice engine — two oscillators and a sub per voice, filter and amp envelopes, LFO, delay, reverb and an arpeggiator. Audio quality and voice count are limited by the browser; the plugin is not. DRIFT, PRISM and ANVIL are effects and are not part of it.

Will these run on my host?

Every plugin ships as VST3, AU, AAX and CLAP, for macOS 11 and later as a Universal binary, Windows 10 and later on x64, and Linux x64 as VST3 and CLAP. The tested host list, and what we do when one of them changes underneath us, is in the docs.
