ANVIL

Transient shaper and saturating bus compressor.

ANVIL does two jobs in one signal path: a transient stage that separates attack from sustain without a detector to fight, and a bus compressor with program-dependent release and a saturation stage that runs after the gain element, not before it. Signal path

Two stages, one pass, in this order

The transient stage runs first, on the untouched signal, so it is shaping the source rather than arguing with a compressor that has already moved. Saturation sits after the gain element, which is why pushing the threshold changes how hard it hits and not how dirty it is.

Inside ANVIL

Six decisions that make it a bus compressor

A dynamics processor you can leave across the mix and stop automating, which is the entire job.

01 / TRANSIENT DETECTION

Attack and sustain, split at the source

Two envelope followers running at different speeds define the split; their difference is the transient. There is no threshold to set, so the stage behaves the same on a quiet verse and a loud chorus, and a snare that drops 6 dB does not stop being a snare.

02 / PROGRAM-DEPENDENT RELEASE

A release that reads the material

Auto release runs two time constants in parallel and weights them by how long the signal has been over threshold, so short hits recover fast and sustained passages recover slowly. Manual release from 30 ms to 1.2 seconds is still there for when you want the pumping.

03 / SATURATION AFTER THE GAIN

Compress first, then colour

The saturation stage sits after the gain element, so lowering the threshold makes the mix hold together harder without making it dirtier. Three curves — a soft transformer knee, a harder Class-A asymmetry, and a clean limiter shape — with drive independent of make-up gain.

04 / SIDECHAIN FILTERING

Stop the kick from running the mix

The detector has its own high-pass from 20 to 800 Hz and low-pass from 1 to 20 kHz, plus an external sidechain input and a listen button so you can hear exactly what the gain element is hearing before you commit to it.

05 / MIX AND PARALLEL

Parallel without the phase argument

A dry/wet control from 0 to 100% with the dry path delay-matched to the wet one, including in oversampled mode. Blend a hard 8:1 setting back under the source and the transients stay where they were instead of arriving twice.

06 / METERING

Numbers you can act on

Gain reduction on a needle with a switchable ballistic, peak and RMS on the same scale, and a readout at 0.1 dB resolution. Hold the meter and it keeps the worst reduction of the pass, so you can find the one bar that is doing all the work.

Questions

Before you strap it across the mix

Is 2.0.1 really free for 1.x owners?

Yes, with the same licence file. Version 2 rebuilt the detector and moved the saturation stage after the gain element, which is a big enough change to earn a major number, and we decided it was not a big enough change to charge existing owners for. Sessions using 1.x load, with a compatibility switch that keeps the old saturation position.

Why is there no threshold on the transient stage?

Because a threshold makes the stage level-dependent, and then a quiet passage stops being shaped at all. ANVIL detects transients from the difference between two envelope followers, which is a ratio rather than a level, so the behaviour holds whether the source is at −24 or −6 dBFS.

Should I use auto release or set it by hand?

On a mix bus, auto. It weights two time constants by how long the signal has stayed over threshold, which is the behaviour people are describing when they say a compressor is transparent. Set it by hand when you want to hear the compressor — a fast manual release is how you get pumping, and pumping is sometimes the point.

Does the mix control cause phase problems?

No. The dry path is delay-matched to the wet path, including in oversampled mode where the wet path picks up 24 samples. At any mix setting the two paths are aligned, so a parallel setting blends level rather than smearing the attack.

Can I use it while tracking?

Yes — with oversampling off, ANVIL reports zero latency in every format. Leave the oversampled mode for mixdown, where the extra 24 samples are compensated by the host and the aliasing in the saturation stage matters more than the round trip.

What does the mid/side link do?

It runs the detector on mid and side separately instead of on a linked stereo sum, so a wide reverb stops pulling the centre down. Channel link is continuous from 0 to 100 %, and mid/side is a switch on top of it rather than a separate mode with its own controls.
