PRISM

4-band spectral filter and formant morpher.

PRISM splits the input into four spectral bands you can move, tilt and resonate independently, then lets you drag the formant structure of one band onto another. Inside PRISM

Six things a four-band split makes possible

A multiband processor that treats the spectrum as material to move around, not as four places to put a compressor.

01 / BAND SPLITTING

Three crossovers you can put anywhere

Each crossover moves freely from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, with slopes from 6 to 48 dB/oct and no minimum spacing — bands are allowed to overlap, and the overlap is where most of the interesting sounds live. Solo and mute per band, and a null test that proves the sum is flat when nothing is engaged.

02 / FORMANT MORPHING

One band's shape, another band's material

The morph stage estimates the spectral envelope of a source band and writes it onto a target band, blended 0 to 100%. Formant shift is decoupled from pitch across ±12 semitones, so you can move the vowel without moving the note underneath it.

03 / SPECTRAL TILT

Per-band slope with a movable pivot

Every band gets a tilt of ±18 dB/oct around a pivot you place inside the band, so darkening the top of a band does not thin the bottom of it. Four tilts in series over a full-range source gives a broadband curve nothing shelving can draw.

04 / RESONANCE

Resonators that will sing if asked

Each band has its own resonator, Q from 0.3 to 60, self-oscillating at the top of the range with key tracking from incoming MIDI. Drive the resonator from the band's own level and it behaves like a filter; drive it from another band and it behaves like an instrument.

05 / MODULATION

Everything on the face is a destination

Two tempo-syncable LFOs, an envelope follower per band, and an external sidechain follower reach every crossover, tilt, morph depth and resonator. Sources and destinations are listed in one table, four columns, and no routing is hidden behind a right-click.

06 / LATENCY

Reported honestly, and avoidable

The spectral path uses a 128-sample analysis window — 2.7 ms at 48 kHz — reported to the host so delay compensation is exact. A tracking mode drops the window entirely for zero latency, at the cost of gentler crossover slopes and no formant morph.

Questions

Buying an unfinished interface

Is it safe to finish a record with a beta?

Yes, with one condition: bounce your stems when you are done, as you would with anything. The DSP, parameter IDs and preset format are frozen at 0.9.0, so a session saved today recalls identically at 1.0. What will change is where the controls sit on screen, not what they do to the audio.

Which parameter ranges are still moving?

The morph depth curve and the resonator Q taper. Both will be remapped so the useful part of the range takes up more of the control, and both remaps preserve the stored value — a preset set to Q 40 today is still Q 40 afterwards, it just sits at a different place on the dial.

Does the morph work on any material?

It works best when the source band has a clear formant structure — voice, brass, a resonant synth — and the target band has broadband energy to shape. Morphing a sine onto a sine gives you a sine. The envelope estimator is order 24, which is enough for vowels and not enough to copy a whole mix.

Why is there latency at all?

Because the formant estimator needs a window to look at. It is 128 samples, reported to the host, and delay compensation handles it. If you need to track through PRISM, the tracking mode removes the window and the latency, and gives up the morph stage and the steepest crossover slopes in exchange.

Do the bands sum flat?

With every band at unity and nothing engaged, yes — the summing stage is phase-aligned and there is a null test in the factory bank that proves it against a bypassed instance. Once you tilt, resonate or morph a band, the sum is no longer flat, which is the entire point.

How do I report something that misbehaves?

Tell us the host and its version, the plugin format, your platform, and what you did in the ten seconds before it happened. Beta reports go to the same people who write the DSP, and the ones that turn into fixes get named in the changelog. The reporting address is on the documentation page.
